1. What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?


This project started with a different topic to the one pushed. I initially built a journey demonstrating the massive height 
differences between normal buildings and the cloud-touching sky-scrapers of the modern age. The interaction was the scroll 
and the information was the height. Then I pivoted; to a completely different topic - The VLOOKUP Excel function. I felt 
that my initial idea didn't relate with something I wanted to learn myself. The VLOOKUP function is something I come back 
to every quarter when I have to file reports for the leadership to assess. Everytime, without fail - I forget what it was 
and have to watch a 15 minute youtube video. This website will be what I come back to instead and save my 10 minutes every quarter. 
With the million of views on the videos on the topic, I hope this website can have even a fraction of that impact. The pivot was my
breakthrough. With more than 10 iterations of the previous website, I was unsatisfied with the outcome. The switch to an idea I was 
personally interested in gave a solid target rather than a moving one.

2. What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

This work gave a new perspective on the importance of having a personal interest in the topic of the work. I was previously 
building something that merely fit the prompt, but had no personal connection to it. When given liberty on the topic, it is much
easier as a software developer to build a solution to one of your own problems. As a software developer, it is key to understand
what the prospective user wants from the product and being a user yourself is the best way to understand what they want. This has 
been demonstrated to work well in one of the most important software development projects - Git, which Linus Torvalds built for himself 
to manage his own projects.
